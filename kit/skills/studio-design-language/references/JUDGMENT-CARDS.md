# Judgment Cards — Inert Reference Appendix

**Deck version:** v1.0 complete-inert
**Date:** 2026-09-17
**Status:** Prepared and complete; not loaded or activated.
**Change note:** v1.0 complete-inert — all 24 indexed cards are drafted, evidence-graded, and remain non-normative until a separate activation decision.

> **Judgment lenses are diagnostic questions, not rules or aesthetic options. Invoke one only to resolve a named project tension. It is invalid if it merely supplies a visual reference; it must produce a project-specific rationale, boundary, and validation condition. Non-use is always permitted.**

This appendix is non-normative decision support. It cannot set tokens, mandate a style, create defaults, or override the existing S/E/H/T doctrine and precedence. It supports the existing deviation protocol only: a lens may help name a tension and formulate a recorded, project-specific rationale and validation condition; it cannot authorize a departure by itself. Evidence tags on cards are limited to H and T because this layer supplies no safety or accessibility floor.

> **Header note for later cultural/appropriation cards:** those cards require the strictest source standard. Stylish critical language is not adequate knowledge of affected communities; claims must be grounded in credible, situated sources and must not substitute design-theory fluency for community knowledge or authority.

## First six cards

### MOV-01 — Grid as negotiated coordination

**Family / evidence tag:** Movement lenses · H

1. **Named trigger / tension:** A project needs alignment, repeatability, or editorial rhythm, but a proposed grid may make every page or touchpoint look interchangeable.
2. **Diagnostic question:** What concrete coordination problem does the grid solve for this content, medium, audience, and production workflow; which visible decisions need to remain free so the concept can speak?
3. **Conditional lens statement:** If the grid is treated as a negotiated instrument for coordinating content rather than as an aesthetic identity, it may clarify hierarchy and production; if it becomes the identity itself, test whether it has homogenized the work.
4. **Precedents as problem / constraint / outcome:** Penguin’s early cover system addressed inconsistent presentation across inexpensive, mass-market books / a distributed publishing and printing workflow / a repeatable visual language. Under Jan Tschichold, Penguin unified title, author, spine, back, symbol, and composition rules; the archive records repeated revised editions of the *Penguin Composition Rules*. These are documented examples of coordination, not proof that a particular grid always improves communication.
5. **Counterexamples / historical failures:** Tschichold’s own later criticism of the rigid New Typography warns against treating asymmetry, sans serif, or a single arrangement as universal answers. A grid copied as a movement signifier can likewise produce uniformity when the communication problem that justified it is absent.
6. **Applicability boundary:** Most useful for editorial, identity, and repeated layouts with real coordination or production constraints. Reassess for responsive interfaces, non-Latin scripts, experimental single artifacts, and brands whose concept depends on refusing regularity. Cross-refs: `LAYOUT-08`, `PRINT-05`.
7. **Prohibited inference:** This card does not prescribe a column count, margin ratio, asymmetry, typeface, or “Swiss” look; it does not make grid use evidence of quality.
8. **S/E/H/T interaction clause:** The tag is H, not a new rank. Existing S/E/H/T precedence remains unchanged. This lens may support a recorded non-S deviation only when the project rationale and validation condition are explicit; it cannot override any existing floor or default by itself.
9. **Action:** Record the coordination problem, the chosen grid variables, and the visible decisions left free. If a grid departure is selected, record why; “no departure warranted” is a valid result.
   **Validation:** At the target medium, check alignment, reading sequence, production repeatability, and whether any departure reads as intentional rather than accidental.
   **Evidence sources:** Design Museum, “Penguin Books,” https://designmuseum.org/penguin-books; University of Bristol Archives, “Penguin Composition Rules,” https://archives.bristol.ac.uk/Record.aspx?id=DM1294%2F16%2F4%2F2&src=CalmView.Catalog; Type Directors Club, Tschichold’s 1959 “Quousque Tandem…” transcription, https://tdc.org/news/jan-tschichold-the-tdc-and-a-mystery/.
   **Confidence:** High for the documented Penguin coordination history and Tschichold’s self-critique; medium for transferring those precedents to a new project.
   **Scope limit:** Precedent-informed diagnosis only; it does not predict audience outcomes or authorize a departure.

### POST-01 — Plurality without stylistic cosplay

**Family / evidence tag:** Postmodern lenses · H

1. **Named trigger / tension:** A brief calls for a critical, plural, vernacular, or anti-authoritarian voice, and a designer proposes deconstruction or appropriation without a defined communicative task.
2. **Diagnostic question:** What authority, assumption, or reading convention is this intervention interrogating, and what will the intended audience gain or lose beyond recognizing a postmodern reference?
3. **Conditional lens statement:** If a plurality-based move exposes a specific cultural or communicative assumption and remains accountable to the audience’s task, it may deepen the concept; if it merely adds fragmentation, competing voices, or a borrowed surface, it is only stylistic cosplay.
4. **Precedents as problem / constraint / outcome:** Weingart’s documented practice investigated typographic tradition, media upheaval, and experimentation rather than supplying a single style. Cranbrook’s *Visible Language* project used progressive disintegration of structure to make post-structuralist theory materially present; the museum describes Katherine McCoy’s recruitment poster as a deconstructivist play in which text can be read as image and image as text. In an AIGA interview, David Carson says the Zapf Dingbats Bryan Ferry interview fit *Ray Gun*’s attitude but also states that designers have a responsibility to the audience and subject matter.
5. **Counterexamples / historical failures:** The Zapf Dingbats interview is a warning that an expressive intervention can erase access to the interview itself. The *Visible Language* scholarship also cautions that “deconstruction” became a label for a style; critical concepts can therefore be reduced to recognizable period gestures detached from the inquiry that gave them purpose.
6. **Applicability boundary:** Applies only where audience, medium, and content can support the proposed interpretive or critical burden. It is not a general permission for illegibility, collage, quotation, or cultural borrowing. Cross-refs: `TYPE-03`, `TYPE-06`, `PRINT-02`, `PRINT-03`.
7. **Prohibited inference:** Do not infer that “postmodern” means chaotic, that illegibility equals critique, or that citing Carson, Weingart, or Cranbrook authorizes their surface vocabulary.
8. **S/E/H/T interaction clause:** Evidence tag H is contextual. The lens cannot create a default, set a token, or override S/E/H/T. It may support an existing deviation record only after the named tension, boundary, and validation are stated.
9. **Action:** Write the assumption being challenged, the audience task retained, and the transformation rather than quotation.
   **Validation:** Use audience comprehension or findability checks, plus critic review for concept-specificity, cultural borrowing, and pastiche.
   **Evidence sources:** Museum für Gestaltung Zürich, “Wolfgang Weingart: Typography in Context,” https://museum-gestaltung.ch/en/article/wolfgang-weingart-typography-context; Cranbrook Art Museum, Katherine McCoy recruitment poster, https://cranbrookartmuseum.org/artwork/katherine-mccoy-posters-the-graduate-program-in-design/; Visible Language, “Deconstruction and Graphic Design: History Meets Theory,” https://journals.uc.edu/index.php/vl/article/view/5612; AIGA Eye on Design, David Carson interview, https://eyeondesign.aiga.org/anti-grid-icon-david-carson-on-why-computers-make-you-lazy-and-indie-mag-design-needs-to-liven-up/.
   **Confidence:** Medium-high for the documented practices and critical distinction between inquiry and style; medium for applying the lens to a new audience.
   **Scope limit:** Historical and critical framing only; it cannot establish that a difficult treatment is appropriate without current audience evidence.

### GOV-01 — Tschichold’s recantation: doctrine must remain revisable

**Family / evidence tag:** Governance · H

1. **Named trigger / tension:** A successful rule set is being presented as universally correct, or repeated consistency is being used to suppress a project-specific exception.
2. **Diagnostic question:** Which problem did the rule solve in its original conditions, what assumptions did it encode, and what evidence would show that those conditions no longer hold here?
3. **Conditional lens statement:** If a rule is retained as a bounded response to a recurring production problem and remains open to evidence, it may institutionalize quality; if it is treated as timeless doctrine, the rule may reproduce the historical failure it was meant to prevent.
4. **Precedents as problem / constraint / outcome:** In his 1959 essay, Tschichold directly criticized his youthful New Typography, including the idea that one typeface and total asymmetry were general remedies; he later argued that typography should serve communication and allow individuality. The Design Museum documents Penguin’s Composition Rules as a response to inconsistent typography across a mass publishing workflow, while the University of Bristol archive records that the rules were revised across later editions. These sources support revisability and bounded governance, not a causal claim that one system produces better design in every context.
5. **Counterexamples / historical failures:** Tschichold’s self-critique is the relevant failure case: a rule set framed as universal can mistake historically situated preferences for necessity. A governance system that cannot record a reasoned exception also makes its own revision impossible.
6. **Applicability boundary:** Applies only to **ELIGIBLE NON-S RULES** under the existing deviation protocol: studio doctrine, brand systems, editorial standards, and repeated workflows where a non-S rule is genuinely disputable. **S floors are never disputable. A named historical precedent never authorizes a departure by itself.** This card does not make every preference equally supported or permit revision to ignore safety, audience evidence, or production reality. Cross-refs: `TYPE-06`, `LAYOUT-08`, `PRINT-05`, and the existing deviation protocol.
7. **Prohibited inference:** Do not infer that recantation invalidates all standards, that consistency is oppressive by definition, or that naming Tschichold permits a departure from an S floor or from any rule that is not eligible for dispute.
8. **S/E/H/T interaction clause:** H is the card’s evidence tag, not a fifth precedence level. The existing S/E/H/T order remains authoritative. This card can prompt a recorded rationale and validation only for an **eligible non-S rule** under the existing deviation protocol. **S floors are never disputable, and a named historical precedent never authorizes a departure by itself.**
9. **Action:** For an eligible non-S rule only, record the original problem, current condition, proposed exception, evidence of fit, and why the existing deviation protocol applies. Do not open a deviation record for an S floor.
   **Validation:** Test at the target audience and medium, retain the decision for review, and verify that the evidence addresses the current project rather than merely naming Tschichold or another precedent.
   **Evidence sources:** Type Directors Club, Jan Tschichold’s “Quousque Tandem…” transcription, https://tdc.org/news/jan-tschichold-the-tdc-and-a-mystery/; Design Museum, “Penguin Books,” https://designmuseum.org/penguin-books; University of Bristol Archives, “Penguin Composition Rules,” https://archives.bristol.ac.uk/Record.aspx?id=DM1294%2F16%2F4%2F2&src=CalmView.Catalog.
   **Confidence:** High for Tschichold’s documented self-critique and Penguin’s revised rules; high for the narrow governance boundary; not evidence of causal quality uplift.
   **Scope limit:** Governance prompt for eligible non-S rules only; it cannot override S floors, precedence, or the existing deviation protocol.

### CULT-01 — Myth, contradiction, and situated identity

**Family / evidence tag:** Cultural branding / semiotics + responsible borrowing · H

1. **Named trigger / tension:** An identity system is expected to make a product signify a larger cultural story, but that story may naturalize ideology or conceal who benefits and who is excluded.
2. **Diagnostic question:** What cultural contradiction does the identity address, whose experience does its myth center, and what material or organizational evidence supports the story beyond its visual performance?
3. **Conditional lens statement:** If a visual myth is treated as a situated hypothesis about an audience’s contradiction and tested against the organization’s conduct and context, it may clarify identity; if it is treated as natural truth, it can turn selective ideology into apparent common sense.
4. **Precedents as problem / constraint / outcome:** Harley-Davidson is useful here as a brand-mythmaking and consumer co-construction precedent, not as proof of universal appeal: Schembri’s three-year Australian ethnography documents a rebellious brand image consumed within a regulated, family-friendly Harley Owners Group context. Dove’s “Real Beauty” case documents an inclusive campaign mission, ordinary-women representation, and the *Evolution* film; the HBS article reports brand sales reaching $2.5 billion by 2007, but that figure does not establish that the campaign caused sales or prove broad cultural resonance. Coca-Cola’s 2009 primary release documents *Open Happiness* as a global integrated platform built around positivity, connection, and local bottling-partner execution; it does not establish campaign reach or cultural success.
5. **Counterexamples / historical failures:** The HBS account records public discussion about the sincerity and motives of Dove’s marketers, showing why representation should not be treated as organizational proof. More generally, branding that borrows a community’s symbols while denying that community agency is a responsible-meaning failure, even when the surface is admired; this card does not claim that any particular community has been adequately represented by the cases above.
6. **Applicability boundary:** Applies to brand identities, campaigns, institutions, and cultural narratives; requires audience/context research and, where relevant, affected-community knowledge. It is weaker for purely informational artifacts where myth is not the brief. Cross-refs: `COLOR-06`, `TYPE-06`, `PRINT-03`.
7. **Prohibited inference:** Do not infer authenticity from documentary style, inclusion from representation count, or ethical legitimacy from sales, attention, campaign reach, or visual polish.
8. **S/E/H/T interaction clause:** H is a contextual diagnostic tag, not authority. The lens cannot set palette, imagery, type, or narrative defaults and cannot override S/E/H/T. It may support a recorded deviation only with contextual rationale, affected-party accountability where relevant, and validation.
9. **Action:** Map denotation, connotation, cultural contradiction, beneficiaries, exclusions, organizational proof, and whose knowledge is authorized to judge the story. Treat reported business or attention outcomes as bounded observations, not causal validation.
   **Validation:** Use situated audience research, affected-community input where relevant, an internal consistency check across touchpoints, and a source review that separates documented conduct from brand rhetoric.
   **Evidence sources:** Quelch and Jocz, Harvard Business School, “Holding a Mirror to Marketing,” https://www.hbs.edu/ris/Publication%20Files/HoldingMirror_Marketing_Quelch_Jocz_2008_aa83223f-1ece-4c37-a6f7-aaa3f9e7cf60.pdf; Schembri, “The Paradox of a Legend,” *Journal of Management & Organization*, https://doi.org/10.5172/jmo.837.14.4.386; Coca-Cola Company, “Open Happiness,” https://investors.coca-colacompany.com/news-events/press-releases/detail/328/open-happiness-and-enjoy-life’s-simple-pleasures-with-coca-cola; Harley-Davidson Museum, “Early Years,” https://www.harley-davidson.com/us/en/experiences/museum/archives/historicstories/earlyyears.html.
   **Confidence:** Medium for the bounded case descriptions; low-to-medium for any inferred cultural effect; no causal sales, reach, or universal-resonance claim is made here.
   **Scope limit:** A situated branding diagnostic only; it cannot settle cultural authority, representational ethics, or affected-community meaning without direct, appropriate input.

### CONFLICT-01 — Novelty versus legibility

**Family / evidence tag:** Conflict cards · H

1. **Named trigger / tension:** A distinctive visual treatment is intended to create attitude or differentiation, but it may slow, distort, or prevent access to the content’s required meaning.
2. **Diagnostic question:** Which part of the message may become harder to find or understand, what audience task justifies that cost, and what is the smallest expressive intervention that carries the concept?
3. **Conditional lens statement:** If the expressive disruption communicates a project-specific attitude while preserving the audience’s required task, it may be a justified novelty; if novelty is the only rationale, legibility loss is an unpriced aesthetic cost.
4. **Precedents as problem / constraint / outcome:** AIGA’s interview with David Carson documents *Ray Gun*’s fractured typography and the Zapf Dingbats Bryan Ferry interview; Carson says it fit the magazine’s attitude while also naming responsibility to the audience and subject matter. The Museum für Gestaltung’s Weingart research documents exploratory typographic practice rather than a universal anti-grid rule. The Design Museum documents Penguin’s quiet consistency as a mass-publishing and production response. These cases show different constraints; they do not establish a transferable outcome for novelty.
5. **Counterexamples / historical failures:** The Zapf Dingbats interview demonstrates novelty becoming total information loss. A grid or anti-grid copied as a movement signifier can likewise displace the brief with historical recognition.
6. **Applicability boundary:** Applies wherever expression and comprehension compete; assess medium, viewing distance, language, disability, stakes, and whether content is optional or essential. Cross-refs: `TYPE-03`, `TYPE-04`, `PRINT-01`, `PRINT-02`, `PRINT-07`.
7. **Prohibited inference:** Do not infer that readable means bland, that anti-legibility is automatically meaningful, or that a successful cultural precedent transfers to a different audience or task.
8. **S/E/H/T interaction clause:** H is a contextual evidence tag, not permission to break an S floor or ignore an E default. Record the tension, departure rationale, and validation under the existing protocol; this card cannot create a novelty allowance.
9. **Action:** Separate essential, supportive, and atmospheric content; apply novelty first to the least costly layer; state the audience task preserved.
   **Validation:** Use target-reader findability and comprehension checks, zoom or distance checks where relevant, and critic review for concept-specificity without pastiche.
   **Evidence sources:** AIGA Eye on Design, David Carson interview and *Ray Gun* documentation, https://eyeondesign.aiga.org/anti-grid-icon-david-carson-on-why-computers-make-you-lazy-and-indie-mag-design-needs-to-liven-up/; Museum für Gestaltung Zürich, “Wolfgang Weingart: Typography in Context,” https://museum-gestaltung.ch/en/article/wolfgang-weingart-typography-context; Design Museum, “Penguin Books,” https://designmuseum.org/penguin-books.
   **Confidence:** High for the documented precedent descriptions; medium for applying their tension to a new audience and medium.
   **Scope limit:** A decision test, not a universal legibility formula or permission to reduce access.

### CRIT-01 — Problem, constraint, outcome: read the precedent

**Family / evidence tag:** Critique prompts · H

1. **Named trigger / tension:** A historical artifact, brand, or movement is cited as a reference, but the team has not shown what problem it solved or why its visual language should matter now.
2. **Diagnostic question:** What problem was the precedent solving, what constraints shaped the choice, what outcome is actually evidenced, and which conditions differ in this project?
3. **Conditional lens statement:** If a precedent is decomposed into problem, constraint, decision, and outcome, it may transfer a reasoning move; if only its appearance transfers, the reference is functioning as decoration or pastiche.
4. **Precedents as problem / constraint / outcome:** Penguin addressed catalogue inconsistency through a repeatable system for mass publishing; the Design Museum documents the template, unified book parts, and Composition Rules. Tschichold’s own 1959 account describes the historical problem and later limits of his New Typography. Dove’s campaign is documented by HBS as a response to narrow beauty stereotypes and as a case in which commercial and social claims were debated; this card makes no causal sales or resonance claim. These cases show why visual evidence alone cannot establish transfer.
5. **Counterexamples / historical failures:** AIGA’s Carson interview documents the Bryan Ferry layout as a case where attitude and audience responsibility were in tension. A single celebrated campaign or artifact can likewise be overgeneralized into a universal identity method while ignoring its audience, power relations, and production conditions.
6. **Applicability boundary:** Use for any cited historical, cultural, or competitor reference, especially at territory selection and critique. It does not replace current audience research, user testing, production proofing, or factual source checking. Cross-refs: `LAYOUT-08`, `PRINT-05`, `PRINT-16`, `COLOR-06`.
7. **Prohibited inference:** Do not infer that resemblance transfers the precedent’s success, that a named movement is a rationale, or that “historical” means evidence-grade.
8. **S/E/H/T interaction clause:** H marks a critique heuristic only. It adds no authority to S/E/H/T, cannot mandate style, and cannot authorize a deviation. It can require a clearer rationale and validation question within the existing deviation protocol.
9. **Action:** For every retained reference, write four lines—problem, constraint, documented outcome or non-outcome, and changed project decision—and one boundary explaining what does not transfer.
   **Validation:** Check that the decision remains legible without the reference’s name, distinguish documented outcomes from interpretation, and run the confusion/pastiche critique.
   **Evidence sources:** Design Museum, “Penguin Books,” https://designmuseum.org/penguin-books; Type Directors Club, Jan Tschichold’s “Quousque Tandem…” transcription, https://tdc.org/news/jan-tschichold-the-tdc-and-a-mystery/; Quelch and Jocz, Harvard Business School, “Holding a Mirror to Marketing,” https://www.hbs.edu/ris/Publication%20Files/HoldingMirror_Marketing_Quelch_Jocz_2008_aa83223f-1ece-4c37-a6f7-aaa3f9e7cf60.pdf; AIGA Eye on Design, David Carson interview, https://eyeondesign.aiga.org/anti-grid-icon-david-carson-on-why-computers-make-you-lazy-and-indie-mag-design-needs-to-liven-up/.
   **Confidence:** High for the source-recording discipline; medium for each precedent’s bounded historical interpretation.
   **Scope limit:** Analytical discipline, not a claim that every precedent has a measurable outcome or that precedent analysis replaces current evidence.

## Remaining cards

### MOV-02 — Functionalism and its situated reader

**Family / evidence tag:** Movement lenses · H

1. **Named trigger / tension:** “Function,” “neutrality,” or “the user” is invoked as if one visual solution serves every reader and setting.
2. **Diagnostic question:** Whose task, language, speed, literacy, device, and institutional situation define function here, and what evidence makes that definition credible?
3. **Conditional lens statement:** If function is specified as a situated relationship among message, reader, medium, and consequence, it can discipline invention; if it is treated as universal neutrality, it hides whose needs and conventions were selected.
4. **Precedents as problem / constraint / outcome:** Kinneir and Calvert developed British road signs from the question of what a driver needs while moving at speed / legal conventions, distance, night reflection, and cost / a coordinated sign system that remained in use with later modifications. The Design Museum’s Penguin account describes cheap mass circulation / repeatable manufacture / standardized covers that still allowed later series-specific redesign. Google Noto brought type designers, linguists, and language communities together / 800 languages and more than 100 writing systems / a cohesive family with script-specific vitality. These are situated responses, not proof of neutrality.
5. **Counterexamples / historical failures:** Kinneir and Calvert’s dropped enlargement of lowercase after tests found only marginal readability improvement shows that a plausible “functional” tweak may not repay its cost. Treating one reader model as universal is the more serious failure this card is designed to expose.
6. **Applicability boundary:** Use for public information, editorial, interface, and identity work where “function” is a stated rationale. Require additional research for multilingual, low-vision, high-stakes, or culturally specific audiences. Cross-refs: `TYPE-04`, `LAYOUT-08`, `PRINT-01`.
7. **Prohibited inference:** Do not infer that modernist simplicity is neutral, that functional intent guarantees functional outcome, or that one test population stands for all readers.
8. **S/E/H/T interaction clause:** H is contextual evidence only. Existing S/E/H/T precedence, including all floors, remains unchanged; this card cannot create a neutral default or authorize a deviation.
9. **Action:** Name the situated reader and task, list constraints, and mark which claims are observed versus inferred.
   **Validation:** Test with representative readers in the actual medium; record failures, non-findings, and unresolved differences rather than collapsing them into “function.”
   **Evidence sources:** Design Museum, “Jock Kinneir and Margaret Calvert,” https://designmuseum.org/designers/jock-kinneir-and-margaret-calvert; Design Museum, “Penguin Books,” https://designmuseum.org/penguin-books; Design Museum, “Google Noto,” https://designmuseum.org/exhibitions/beazley-designs-of-the-year/digital-20x/google-noto.
   **Confidence:** High for the described cases; medium for transfer to a new reader group.
   **Scope limit:** A reader-situating prompt, not a usability standard or universal theory of function.

### MOV-03 — Modernist transparency versus expressive voice

**Family / evidence tag:** Movement lenses · H

1. **Named trigger / tension:** Typography must support quick comprehension yet also signal stance, authorship, or cultural position.
2. **Diagnostic question:** Which information must disappear into effortless access, and where can visible voice operate without making the reader solve the wrong problem?
3. **Conditional lens statement:** If transparency is allocated to essential content and expression to a bounded layer, clarity and voice can coexist; if either becomes total, the system may erase authorship or erase the message.
4. **Precedents as problem / constraint / outcome:** Kinneir and Calvert reduced road-sign appearance to “maximum sense and minimum cost” / speed, distance, legal coding, and night reflection / a legible coordinated system. Crouwel used structured frameworks and readable sans serif support around modular experimental letterforms / exhibition communication plus technological change / expressive forms whose legibility was bounded by surrounding information. Cranbrook’s McCoy poster paired readable oppositional words with a deconstructivist image-text relation / recruitment communication and conceptual play / a poster the museum describes as an icon of postmodern graphic design. None proves a general clarity-expression formula.
5. **Counterexamples / historical failures:** The Zapf Dingbats interview documented by AIGA is a counterexample where attitude can overwhelm content. A purely transparent system can also make the author’s position invisible; that inference requires project evidence, not a historical citation.
6. **Applicability boundary:** Applies to editorial, exhibition, campaign, and identity typography with distinct essential and expressive layers. Reassess for emergency information, low-literacy contexts, and languages whose typographic conventions differ from the precedent.
7. **Prohibited inference:** Do not infer that expression belongs only in display type, or that “modernist” clarity and “postmodern” voice are mutually exclusive.
8. **S/E/H/T interaction clause:** H does not outrank existing S/E/H/T. It cannot lower an accessibility floor, prescribe a typeface, or turn stylistic opposition into a default.
9. **Action:** Classify content by consequence, assign expressive risk to the least consequential layer, and state the voice claim in plain language.
   **Validation:** Check findability and comprehension first, then ask whether the expressive layer is noticed as stance rather than noise or period imitation.
   **Evidence sources:** Design Museum, “Jock Kinneir and Margaret Calvert,” https://designmuseum.org/designers/jock-kinneir-and-margaret-calvert; Design Museum, “Wim Crouwel,” https://designmuseum.org/designers/wim-crouwel; Cranbrook Art Museum, Katherine McCoy poster, https://cranbrookartmuseum.org/artwork/katherine-mccoy-posters-the-graduate-program-in-design/; AIGA, David Carson interview, https://eyeondesign.aiga.org/anti-grid-icon-david-carson-on-why-computers-make-you-lazy-and-indie-mag-design-needs-to-liven-up/.
   **Confidence:** High for source descriptions; medium for the proposed allocation test.
   **Scope limit:** A layer-allocation diagnostic, not a claim that one hierarchy works for every audience.

### MOV-04 — Historical inheritance across scripts

**Family / evidence tag:** Movement lenses · H

1. **Named trigger / tension:** A Latin-derived grid, type hierarchy, or reading direction is being carried into multilingual or culturally different reading conditions as if structure transfers unchanged.
2. **Diagnostic question:** What is inherited—the task, the hierarchy, the production constraint, or merely the Latin surface—and who has authority to judge whether the receiving script is being served?
3. **Conditional lens statement:** If the transferable problem is separated from the source script’s conventions and developed with script expertise, historical inheritance can become a respectful system; if Latin form is treated as neutral scaffolding, it can distort language and authority.
4. **Precedents as problem / constraint / outcome:** Google Noto addressed broad script coverage / collaboration among type designers, linguists, and language communities / a family spanning 800 languages and more than 100 writing systems. Amorepacific’s Seoul signage addressed English, Chinese, and Korean in one architectural environment / visual consistency plus local wayfinding / a new Latin typeface alongside letterforms, numerals, and pictograms. The British Museum’s Rosetta Stone account records one decree in hieroglyphic, Demotic, and Greek / multiple administrative and religious audiences / parallel scripts that enabled later decipherment. These cases document different multilingual problems; they do not establish a universal script hierarchy.
5. **Counterexamples / historical failures:** The British Museum notes that Libyco-Berber terminology, chronology, direction, and variants remain contested; collapsing those distinctions into a decorative “ancient script” is a historical and cultural failure. A Latin grid copied without script review is the corresponding contemporary risk.
6. **Applicability boundary:** Use only with project-specific script, language, reading-direction, and community expertise. For culturally governed scripts or living communities, escalate to qualified language/script practitioners; this card never authorizes a design decision alone.
7. **Prohibited inference:** Do not infer that visual cohesion requires identical geometry, that a bilingual precedent licenses borrowing, or that historical resemblance demonstrates cultural accuracy.
8. **S/E/H/T interaction clause:** H is not a permission layer. Existing S/E/H/T precedence remains unchanged; script accessibility and community authority cannot be traded away for visual consistency.
9. **Action:** Inventory scripts, directions, language roles, and authority; commission script-specific review before locking hierarchy or transliteration.
   **Validation:** Test native-language reading, hierarchy, spacing, and meaning in context; record unresolved linguistic or cultural questions as escalation, not as solved design rationale.
   **Evidence sources:** Design Museum, “Google Noto,” https://designmuseum.org/exhibitions/beazley-designs-of-the-year/digital-20x/google-noto; Design Museum, “Amorepacific architectural branding,” https://designmuseum.org/exhibitions/beazley-designs-of-the-year/graphic-2019/amorepacific-architectural-branding; British Museum, “Rosetta Stone,” https://www.britishmuseum.org/blog/everything-you-ever-wanted-know-about-rosetta-stone; British Museum, “Written in stone: Libyco-Berber scripts,” https://africanrockart.britishmuseum.org/thematic/written-in-stone/.
   **Confidence:** High for documented source conditions; medium for transfer; low for any community-specific interpretation without local authority.
   **Scope limit:** A research-and-escalation prompt, never a script or cultural decision rule.

### POST-02 — Constraint as an engine of experimentation

**Family / evidence tag:** Postmodern lenses · H

1. **Named trigger / tension:** A project names a movement style to produce experimental energy, while its actual opportunity may be a material, technical, temporal, or production constraint.
2. **Diagnostic question:** What constraint is materially real, and what experiment does it make possible that a borrowed movement surface would not?
3. **Conditional lens statement:** If the constraint changes the form of inquiry and the result remains accountable to the task, it can generate original experimentation; if the constraint is only narrated after choosing a style, it is retrospective decoration.
4. **Precedents as problem / constraint / outcome:** Crouwel’s Design Museum record links his modular letterforms to technological and exhibition conditions / structured frameworks and readable support / experimental forms bounded by communication. Penguin’s Pocket Penguins combined a tight budget and anniversary timing / small format, low price, and inherited identity / a simple typographic series that preserved some heritage without copying every prior cover. McCoy’s poster used a perforated postcard format and bifurcated recruitment message / a physical promotional object / image-text instability tied to the institutional split.
5. **Counterexamples / historical failures:** A deconstruction label applied without a problem can become stylistic cosplay, as the Cranbrook artifact is often cited for its visible surface rather than its recruitment and conceptual constraints. A made-up limitation that cannot be tested is not a meaningful constraint.
6. **Applicability boundary:** Applies to print, motion, digital, and identity work where a real constraint can be named and observed. It does not justify sacrificing essential content or cultural accountability.
7. **Prohibited inference:** Do not infer that scarcity is automatically generative, that a visibly rough result is experimental, or that a movement reference proves transformation.
8. **S/E/H/T interaction clause:** H remains contextual and non-normative. No constraint permits an S-floor violation or overrides existing S/E/H/T precedence.
9. **Action:** Write the constraint, its mechanism, the experiment it enables, and the task it cannot compromise.
   **Validation:** Prototype against the constraint; compare the result to a non-experimental baseline and record what the constraint actually changed.
   **Evidence sources:** Design Museum, “Wim Crouwel,” https://designmuseum.org/designers/wim-crouwel; Design Museum, “Penguin Books,” https://designmuseum.org/penguin-books; Cranbrook Art Museum, Katherine McCoy poster, https://cranbrookartmuseum.org/artwork/katherine-mccoy-posters-the-graduate-program-in-design/.
   **Confidence:** Medium-high for precedent descriptions; medium for applying the constraint test.
   **Scope limit:** A process diagnostic, not a recommendation to introduce artificial limitations.

### POST-03 — Anti-legibility as situated communication

**Family / evidence tag:** Postmodern lenses · H

1. **Named trigger / tension:** Difficulty, fragmentation, or unreadability is proposed to communicate alienation, critique, intimacy, speed, or institutional distrust.
2. **Diagnostic question:** Who is meant to experience the difficulty, what do they need to recover, and what evidence shows the friction communicates rather than merely blocks?
3. **Conditional lens statement:** If friction is localized, intentional, and interpretable within the audience’s task, it may communicate a condition; if the audience cannot identify what to do or what is being questioned, anti-legibility is task failure.
4. **Precedents as problem / constraint / outcome:** McCoy’s poster made text-image relations unstable while retaining oppositional words and a recruitment purpose / institutional division and physical poster format / a documented deconstructivist composition. Crouwel used radical modular letterforms with easily read sans-serif support / experimentation within exhibition communication / expressive typography with a surrounding legibility scaffold. Carson’s *Ray Gun* example shows an attitude-driven intervention / magazine voice / a documented case where responsibility to subject matter remained a stated concern.
5. **Counterexamples / historical failures:** The Zapf Dingbats interview makes the failure concrete: the interview is no longer findable as prose. The risk is not that all difficulty is bad; it is that the card is invoked after the task has already been abandoned.
6. **Applicability boundary:** Not for emergency, instructional, legal, medical, or essential navigation content unless the affected task remains independently available. For culturally specific expressions of opacity or refusal, obtain situated input rather than theorizing on behalf of a community.
7. **Prohibited inference:** Do not infer that obscurity equals depth, that confusion is evidence of critique, or that a successful precedent transfers its audience contract.
8. **S/E/H/T interaction clause:** H cannot lower existing floors or make non-legibility a default. Existing S/E/H/T precedence and deviation protocol remain binding.
9. **Action:** Define the intended friction, preserved task, audience, and recovery path; escalate if community meaning is being interpreted externally.
   **Validation:** Run comprehension, findability, and recovery checks with representative users; verify that the friction is noticed as intentional and not simply missed information.
   **Evidence sources:** Cranbrook Art Museum, Katherine McCoy poster, https://cranbrookartmuseum.org/artwork/katherine-mccoy-posters-the-graduate-program-in-design/; Design Museum, “Wim Crouwel,” https://designmuseum.org/designers/wim-crouwel; AIGA, David Carson interview, https://eyeondesign.aiga.org/anti-grid-icon-david-carson-on-why-computers-make-you-lazy-and-indie-mag-design-needs-to-liven-up/.
   **Confidence:** High for the documented artifacts; medium for the transfer test.
   **Scope limit:** Situated communication diagnosis only; not a license for inaccessible output.

### POST-04 — Authorship, quotation, and pastiche

**Family / evidence tag:** Postmodern lenses · H

1. **Named trigger / tension:** A reference is being copied or closely imitated, but the project has not said what transformation, authorship, or ethical position makes the quotation necessary.
2. **Diagnostic question:** What is being quoted, from whom, for what project-specific reason, and what changes in meaning when it enters this medium and audience?
3. **Conditional lens statement:** If quotation is named, transformed, credited where appropriate, and tested for audience confusion, it can produce situated dialogue; if recognition is the only payoff, it is pastiche.
4. **Precedents as problem / constraint / outcome:** Penguin’s Great Ideas series used historical lettering styles / a need to connect books to the periods of their texts while retaining a rigorous series palette / diverse expressive covers within a coherent identity. Facetti’s Penguin Crime redesign retained hierarchy and green coding while adding dramatic image-making / inherited series recognition and contemporary renewal / a documented redesign adopted in variations for other series. McCoy’s poster made institutional oppositions visible through an image-text quotation of deconstructivist ideas / recruitment context / a marked departure from earlier work.
5. **Counterexamples / historical failures:** Penguin’s own loss of design edge before Facetti, as described by the Design Museum, warns that heritage can become a constraint when not renewed. A surface copy can preserve recognition while losing the original problem and authorship.
6. **Applicability boundary:** Requires provenance and rights review for living creators, communities, and protected marks. Cultural quotation requires affected-community input; this card cannot determine legitimacy.
7. **Prohibited inference:** Do not infer that quotation is automatically critique, that historical style is public property, or that adding a new color or crop creates transformation.
8. **S/E/H/T interaction clause:** H is contextual only; existing S/E/H/T precedence and rights, safety, and accessibility obligations remain unchanged.
9. **Action:** Build a provenance note: source, author/community, element quoted, transformation, purpose, credit, and confusion risk. Escalate unresolved authority.
   **Validation:** Remove the reference name and test whether the project rationale still stands; run confusion, attribution, and audience review.
   **Evidence sources:** Design Museum, “Penguin Books,” https://designmuseum.org/penguin-books; Cranbrook Art Museum, Katherine McCoy poster, https://cranbrookartmuseum.org/artwork/katherine-mccoy-posters-the-graduate-program-in-design/.
   **Confidence:** High for the Penguin and Cranbrook descriptions; medium for the ethical transfer test.
   **Scope limit:** Quotation diagnostic, not a copyright or cultural-approval determination.

### GOV-02 — Penguin governance, not Penguin aesthetic

1. **Named trigger / tension:** A team wants a shared look because a historical system appears coherent, while the real need may be shared production decisions.
2. **Diagnostic question:** Which governance problem needs agreement—ownership, review, versioning, contribution, or quality—and which visual choices should remain project-specific?
3. **Conditional lens statement:** If governance is defined as roles, evidence, review, and controlled evolution, a system can coordinate without homogenizing; if governance is reduced to copying the reference look, it becomes aesthetic policing.
4. **Precedents as problem / constraint / outcome:** Penguin’s Composition Rules responded to inconsistent typography across mass production / many printers and titles / repeatable standards. 7. **Prohibited inference:** Do not infer that a strict visual template is mature governance, or that deviation is disobedience.
8. **S/E/H/T interaction clause:** H adds no rank. Existing S/E/H/T precedence remains unchanged; this card cannot create defaults or loosen floors.
9. **Action:** Record owners, contribution path, review cadence, exception path, version boundary, and what the system deliberately does not standardize.
   **Validation:** Review a real extension and a real exception; check whether the protocol produces explainable decisions without requiring visual imitation.

### GOV-03 — Exception records as institutional memory

**Family / evidence tag:** Governance · H

1. **Named trigger / tension:** Teams repeatedly depart from a rule, but the reasons, evidence, and outcomes are not preserved for later review.
2. **Diagnostic question:** Is this an exception, a missing system capability, or evidence that the rule should be revised—and what future observation would distinguish them?
3. **Conditional lens statement:** If exceptions preserve trigger, rationale, boundary, validation, and outcome, they can teach a system; if they are undocumented, each project reopens the same argument and drift becomes invisible.
4. **Precedents as problem / constraint / outcome:** Penguin’s archive records Composition Rules and later revised editions / evolving publishing and production needs / a system that could be modified rather than treated as timeless. ### CULT-02 — Exchange, appropriation, and authority to judge

**Family / evidence tag:** Cultural branding / semiotics + responsible borrowing · H

1. **Named trigger / tension:** A brand proposes symbols, vernacular, dress, language, or subcultural codes associated with another community.
2. **Diagnostic question:** Who made or governs the sign, what power relation structures the exchange, who benefits, who bears risk, and who has authority to judge the use?
3. **Conditional lens statement:** If the project treats borrowing as a situated power and authority question, seeks affected-community input, and can document consent/credit/benefit where appropriate, it may proceed to project-specific research; if it relies on designer intent or stylish semiotics alone, it must stop and escalate.
4. **Precedents as problem / constraint / outcome:** Westberg’s peer-reviewed study of Sámi jeans analyzes how materials, language, and iconography commodify “Sáminess” / global fashion and authenticity claims / authenticity is shown as a power-laden discourse, not a neutral visual property. The *Journal of Consumer Research* study of cultural difference examines international K-pop fans’ self-authorization strategies / unequal cultural exchange and contested imagery / four consumer strategies, while explicitly studying consumer discourse rather than granting community authority. These sources support escalation, not a verdict about any new project.
5. **Counterexamples / historical failures:** The Sámi case warns against a commodity claiming local authenticity through signs while relocating meaning for a global market. The K-pop study warns that consumers can rationalize continued consumption; consumer permission is not community consent.
6. **Applicability boundary:** **Strictest standard:** any living community, sacred/restricted sign, Indigenous or minoritized cultural expression, dialect, hairstyle, garment, ritual, or subcultural code requires affected-community input or project-specific research. If authority, consent, credit, or benefit cannot be established, do not make the design decision from this card.
7. **Prohibited inference:** Do not infer appreciation from positive intent, visual research, payment to a generic consultant, audience liking, or a community’s internal diversity; do not infer authority from a peer-reviewed article.
8. **S/E/H/T interaction clause:** H is contextual and cannot authorize borrowing, settle cultural meaning, or override S/E/H/T. The action is escalation, not a design permission.
9. **Action:** Pause the borrowing decision; identify affected communities and authority structures; commission situated research and/or direct input, with credit, consent, compensation, and benefit questions documented.
   **Validation:** Affected-community or qualified project-specific review must answer the authority and harm questions; absent that evidence, retain the escalation and do not proceed.
   **Evidence sources:** Westberg, “Establishing authenticity and commodifying difference,” *Visual Communication*, https://doi.org/10.1177/1470357219896819; Lin et al., “Between Cultural Appreciation and Cultural Appropriation,” *Journal of Consumer Research*, https://doi.org/10.1093/jcr/ucad022.
   **Confidence:** High for the bounded findings of these peer-reviewed studies; low for any project-specific cultural judgment without situated authority.
   **Scope limit:** Escalation prompt only; no universal appropriation test and no community meaning inferred.

**Family / evidence tag:** Cultural branding / semiotics + responsible borrowing · H

### CULT-04 — Subcultural codes after commodification

### CRIT-02 — Whose “good design” is being universalized?

**Family / evidence tag:** Critique prompts · H

1. **Named trigger / tension:** Taste, rationality, neutrality, clarity, or “good design” is stated without naming the audience, power context, or excluded reader.
2. **Diagnostic question:** Who benefits from this definition, whose labor or knowledge made it possible, and what evidence would show that it serves the intended audience rather than the evaluator’s habit?
3. **Conditional lens statement:** If evaluative language is situated in audience, task, power, and evidence, critique can become accountable; if it remains universal, it turns preference into doctrine.
4. **Precedents as problem / constraint / outcome:** Kinneir and Calvert’s road signs addressed drivers at speed and used testing and legal conventions / public infrastructure / a coordinated system still in use with modifications. ### CRIT-03 — Rationale or retrospective decoration?

**Family / evidence tag:** Critique prompts · H

1. **Named trigger / tension:** A historical or theoretical rationale is offered after the visual decision but does not alter the decision, boundary, or validation.

### CRIT-04 — Counterexample before confidence

**Family / evidence tag:** Critique prompts · H

1. **Named trigger / tension:** One celebrated artifact, campaign, or movement is being generalized into a doctrine claim.
2. **Diagnostic question:** What nearby case failed, changed direction, or required different conditions—and would that counterexample alter this project’s choice?
3. **Conditional lens statement:** If a precedent is paired with a credible counterexample and a condition of transfer, confidence can become bounded; if only successes are collected, the reference set is advocacy, not research.
4. **Precedents as problem / constraint / outcome:** Penguin’s history includes successful coordination, later loss of edge, and subsequent renewal / changing publishing conditions / evidence that systems age and can be revised. Tschichold’s 1959 self-critique is a direct counterexample to universal New Typography claims / historical shift and reflection / doctrine made revisable. NN/G’s maturity model rejects a linear universal endpoint / organizations regress or remain deliberately lean / multidimensional assessment.
5. **Counterexamples / historical failures:** The cases above are themselves the required counterexample structure: a system can be coherent and still become stale; a famous method can later be criticized by its author; a mature organization can regress. No success claim transfers without checking conditions.
6. **Applicability boundary:** Applies to critique and strategy claims about history, style, governance, audience, and outcomes. For cultural claims, the counterexample does not substitute for affected-community authority.
7. **Prohibited inference:** Do not infer that every success needs an opposite mirror case of equal scale, or that one failure disproves a bounded method.
8. **S/E/H/T interaction clause:** H supplies an evidence-demanding prompt only; it cannot change S/E/H/T precedence or authorize a deviation.
9. **Action:** Add one credible counterexample, state what condition differs, and revise confidence and scope before retaining the claim.
   **Validation:** Ask whether the counterexample changes the project decision; if not, explain why in project-specific terms rather than dismissing it as irrelevant.
   **Evidence sources:** Design Museum, “Penguin Books,” https://designmuseum.org/penguin-books; Type Directors Club, Tschichold’s “Quousque Tandem…” transcription, https://tdc.org/news/jan-tschichold-the-tdc-and-a-mystery/; NN/G, “Design-System Maturity,” https://www.nngroup.com/articles/design-system-maturity/.
   **Confidence:** High for the cited counterexample discipline; medium for transfer to an individual project.
   **Scope limit:** Confidence calibration prompt, not a statistical method or proof of causality.
